Method and implementation notes

Experimental protocol

  • Problem sizes: N = 1,000 and 10,000, with 50% prefilled before each run.
  • Workloads: 0C-50I-50D, 50C-25I-25D, and 100C-0I-0D.
  • Thread counts: 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16; five repetitions per configuration.
  • Workers use a shared 10-second deadline based on System.nanoTime(). Prefill, trace generation, worker creation, and CSV output are outside the workload. Elapsed time is recorded through worker completion; six Q3 runs exceeded 10.05 seconds, with a maximum of 10.668 seconds. Those measured durations were retained in the calculations.
  • Throughput is completed operation calls divided by the measured elapsed seconds, in millions of operations per second (MOPS). Speedup is the ratio of average throughput at P threads to the matching one-thread average.

Algorithms and linearization points

  • Q1 list: the lazy list inserts at the protected predecessor-link update and deletes at the logical mark; unsuccessful insert/delete and contains linearize at the decision made in the validated, locked window. The non-blocking version publishes immutable doubly linked snapshots with one state CAS; successful updates linearize at that CAS, while unsuccessful updates and contains linearize at the observed snapshot read. It has no transient mark/help/unlink phase: delete omits the key from a new version and atomically publishes that version. In-flight readers may finish on an older immutable version. Copy-on-write updates are O(N), a deliberate correctness-first tradeoff rather than the Sundell-Tsigas linked-node algorithm.
  • Q2 stack: lock-based push/pop linearize at the protected top mutation, with empty-pop and peek at the protected top observation. Treiber push/pop linearize at a successful top CAS; empty-pop and peek linearize at the atomic top observation.
  • Q3 queue: lock-based enqueue/dequeue linearize at the protected link/head update, with empty-dequeue and peek at the protected head observation. Michael-Scott enqueue linearizes at the successful tail.next CAS and successful dequeue at the head CAS; empty-dequeue and peek linearize at the validated head/next observation.
  • Q4 elimination stack: operations that use the central stack linearize at the Treiber top CAS (or the top observation for empty-pop/peek). A matched push/pop pair linearizes at the successful WAITING-to-MATCHED CAS on its shared elimination offer.

Every run completed and passed its post-run structure check. The base stress and invariant suite ran on all three hosts. After the sentinel change, the extended Q1 suite passed on Device 1 and covered empty and populated lists, duplicate keys, Integer.MIN_VALUE and Integer.MAX_VALUE insertion/lookup/deletion, concurrent list membership, stack value preservation, per-producer queue order, elimination-stack value exchange, and mixed-operation stress. Both lists use long-valued sentinels outside the int range. These checks are not a formal linearizability proof. CAS retries were not counted, so contention is discussed qualitatively.

Interpretation and limitations

Compare each curve within its host and matching implementation, size, and workload. Read-heavy structures may scale differently from update-heavy structures: lock-based paths serialize on shared locks, while non-blocking paths can experience failed CAS retries and cache-coherence traffic on hot references. More threads can improve aggregate throughput, saturate, or regress depending on the critical path and scheduling overhead.

The Q1 snapshot design trades update cost for immutable versions and a single CAS publication. The lock-based stack and queue serialize updates, while Treiber and Michael-Scott avoid a global lock but still contend on shared atomic references. The measurements reflect these tradeoffs for the tested workloads; they should not be generalized beyond this setup.

Raw operation counts, elapsed times, and run flags are included in the CSV files. Averages and speedups were calculated from those rows. The three machines reported the same CPU model, but each question's speedup is based only on runs from its assigned host.
